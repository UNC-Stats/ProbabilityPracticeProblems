    2. Joint Distributions: Conditional PMFs and Independence.

    An e-commerce platform maps user behavior across an active shopping session. Let the discrete random variable \(X\) denote the user’s browsing engagement tier (\(1, 2, \text{ or } 3\)), and let the discrete random variable \(Y\) denote their final purchase category tier (\(1, 2, \text{ or } 3\)).
    🔗
    Browsing engagement tier definitions:
    🔗
    • Tier 1: Shallow Engagement (Bounce / Skim): The user clicks a direct link, views a single landing page, or briefly skims a product description for under a minute before either checking out or exiting.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Tier 2: Moderate Engagement (Active Browsing): The user actively navigates the site. They use the search bar, apply filtering tags, compare 2 to 5 different products, or read a few customer reviews.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Tier 3: Deep Engagement (Immersive Research): The user goes down a rabbit hole. They interact with 3D product models, watch video demonstrations, read long-form support wikis, customize item configurations, or post questions in the community forum.
      🔗
      🔗
    Purchase category tier definitions:
    🔗
    • Tier 1: Low-Value / Essential Items: Inexpensive items like single phone cases, charging cables, basic stickers, or simple clearance items (e.g., spending under $15).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Tier 2: Mid-Value / Standard Merchandise: Mid-range purchases like standard hoodies, board games, vinyl records, or mid-tier apparel (e.g., spending $15 to $75).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Tier 3: High-Value / Premium Goods: High-end items like premium collector’s editions, electronics, high-end mechanical keyboards, or leather outerwear items (e.g., spending over $75).
      🔗
      🔗

    🔗
    \(X\)
    \(Y\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(1\) \(0.15\) \(0.05\) \(0.05\)
    \(2\) \(0.05\) \(0.35\) \(0.10\)
    \(3\) \(0.10\) \(0.10\) \(0.05\)
    🔗

      Random variables \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent.
      🔗

    • For two discrete random variables to be independent, the multiplication property must hold for every single cell intersection in the grid layout: \(p(x, y) = P(X = x) \cdot P(Y = y)\text{.}\)
      Let us check the first upper-left cell entry at position \((1, 1)\text{:}\)
      • Actual intersection: \(p(1, 1) = 0.15\)
        🔗

      • Marginal column total for \(X = 1\text{:}\) \(P(X=1) = 0.15 + 0.05 + 0.10 = 0.30\)
        🔗

      • Marginal row total for \(Y = 1\text{:}\) \(P(Y=1) = 0.15 + 0.05 + 0.05 = 0.25\)
        🔗
        🔗
      Evaluating the independence product:
      \begin{equation*} P(X=1) \cdot P(Y=1) = 0.30 \cdot 0.25 = 0.075 \end{equation*}
      Because \(p(1, 1) = 0.15 \neq 0.075\text{,}\) the conditions for statistical independence are violated. Alternatively, we can see that the conditional profile found in Part A (\(P(Y=1 \mid X=2) = 0.10\)) does not match the baseline marginal rate (\(P(Y=1) = 0.25\)). Therefore, the answer is NO.

      Let us check the first upper-left cell entry at position \((1, 1)\text{:}\)
      • Actual intersection: \(p(1, 1) = 0.15\)
        🔗

        🔗

    🔗
    (c)
    Calculate the covariance, \(\text{Cov}(X, Y)\text{,}\) for this joint probability distribution to evaluate the directional relationship between browsing engagement and purchase tiers (i.e. how they vary together).
    🔗
    • \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0.00\)
    • Incorrect. A covariance of exactly zero implies that the random variables have no linear relationship, which is a property of independent variables. As verified in Part B, these variables are dependent, so their covariance is non-zero.
    • \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0.05\)
    • Correct! To calculate covariance, we use the identity formula: \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E(XY) - E(X)E(Y)\text{.}\)
      • Step 1: Find the expected values from the row and column totals: \(E(X) = 1(0.30) + 2(0.50) + 3(0.20) = 1.90\) and \(E(Y) = 1(0.25) + 2(0.50) + 3(0.25) = 2.00\text{.}\)
      • Step 2: Compute the joint expectation by summing \(x \cdot y \cdot p(x,y)\) for all 9 cells: \(E(XY) = (1\cdot1\cdot0.15) + (1\cdot2\cdot0.05) + \dots + (3\cdot3\cdot0.05) = 3.85\text{.}\)
      • Step 3: Substitute into the identity formula: \(\text{Cov}(X,Y) = 3.85 - (1.90 \cdot 2.00) = 3.85 - 3.80 = 0.05\text{.}\)
      The positive value indicates that higher browsing engagement is weakly associated with higher purchase tiers.
    • \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 3.85\)
    • Incorrect. This is the value of the joint expectation \(E(XY)\) alone. You still need to complete the formula steps by subtracting the product of the individual expected values, \(E(X)E(Y)\text{.}\)
    • \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = -0.05\)
    • Incorrect. This value indicates a negative relationship, but our joint expectation product \(E(XY) = 3.85\) is strictly larger than the individual expected product baseline \(E(X)E(Y) = 3.80\text{,}\) leading to a positive net difference.
    • \(\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0.15\)
    • Incorrect. This looks like an tracking calculation error when evaluating the sum of the coordinate combinations for the joint expectation \(E(XY)\text{.}\) Re-verify your arithmetic products for each grid position.

    4. Joint Distributions: Esports Team Performance Metrics.

    An analytics platform for competitive multiplayer video games reviews performance data from a professional tournament. Let the discrete random variable \(X\) denote the tactical communication pings sent by a team’s primary attacker during a round (\(X \in \{1, 2, 3\}\)). Let the discrete random variable \(Y\) denote the number of heavy protective defense walls deployed by their support teammate during the same match loop (\(Y \in \{1, 2, 3\}\)).
    🔗
    The joint probability distribution, \(p(x, y)\text{,}\) is shown below:
    🔗
    \(X\) (Pings Sent)
    \(Y\) (Walls Deployed) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(1\) \(0.10\) \(0.15\) \(0.05\)
    \(2\) \(0.05\) \(0.30\) \(0.15\)
    \(3\) \(0.05\) \(0.10\) \(0.05\)
    🔗
    (a)
    🔗
    (b)
    Compute the joint expected value of the product of the two cooperation variables, written as \(E(XY)\text{.}\) Multiplying Pings Sent (\(X\)) and Defensive Walls Deployed (\(Y\))gives us a measure of Team Cohesion and Synchronization, where low values represent Unsynchronized or Disconnected Play, moderate values represent standard, balanced play, and high values represent High-Intensity Strategic Synergy.
    🔗
    • \(E(XY) = 2.05\)
    • Incorrect. This is just the marginal expectation \(E(X)\) calculated in Part A. To find \(E(XY)\text{,}\) you must cross-multiply every single cell index coordinate weight across all 9 regions of the joint matrix.
    • \(E(XY) = 3.995\)
    • Incorrect. This is the result of multiplying \(E(X) \cdot E(Y)\text{.}\) This shortcut identity formula only holds true if the two random variables are completely independent. Because ping actions and defensive wall positioning are dependent in this matrix, you must calculate the sum of products cell-by-cell.
    • \(E(XY) = 4.05\)
    • Correct! To evaluate the joint expectation of a product, we sum the product of the coordinates multiplied by their corresponding cell weight across all 9 positions in the matrix grid: \(E(XY) = \sum \sum x \cdot y \cdot p(x,y)\text{.}\)
      \begin{align*} E(XY) \amp = (1 \cdot 1 \cdot 0.10) + (2 \cdot 1 \cdot 0.15) + (3 \cdot 1 \cdot 0.05)\\ \amp + (1 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.05) + (2 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.30) + (3 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.15)\\ \amp + (1 \cdot 3 \cdot 0.05) + (2 \cdot 3 \cdot 0.10) + (3 \cdot 3 \cdot 0.05)\\ \amp = 0.10 + 0.30 + 0.15 + 0.10 + 1.20 + 0.90 + 0.15 + 0.60 + 0.45 = 4.05 \end{align*}
      The joint expected value of the product is exactly 4.05.
    • \(E(XY) = 1.00\)
    • Incorrect. This is the sum of the raw probability weights inside the grid. The expected value requires scaling those baseline weights by their structural coordinate indices.
    • \(E(XY) = 4.50\)
    • Incorrect. This looks like a tracking index error where row and column headings were accidentally offset during the evaluation step. Re-verify the cross-multiplication matrix pairs.
