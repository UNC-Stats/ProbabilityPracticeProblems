    • \(30,000\) houses
    • Incorrect. It looks like you divided the entire $3,000 revenue target by the probability parameter \(p = 0.10\text{.}\) Make sure to convert your revenue target to a target number of successful sales (\(r\)) first.

    • Correct! First, determine the number of target successes (\(r\)) needed to reach the financial goal:
      \begin{equation*} r = \frac{\$3000 \text{ target}}{\$250 \text{ per job}} = 12 \text{ agreements} \end{equation*}
      Next, apply the expected value function using our parameter count of \(r = 12\) and success probability \(p = 0.10\text{:}\)
      \begin{equation*} E(Y) = \frac{r}{p} = \frac{12}{0.10} = 120 \text{ houses} \end{equation*}
    • \(1.2\) houses
    • Incorrect. It looks like you multiplied the target sales number of 12 by the probability parameter \(p = 0.10\) instead of dividing by it. The expected number of trials will always be larger than the target number of successes when \(p \lt 1\text{.}\)

    🔗
