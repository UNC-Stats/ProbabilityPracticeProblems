    Section 5.5 Hypergeometric Random Variable

    By completing the problems provided below, you will gain experience using the Hypergeometric random variable. Specifically, you will practice:
    • Identifying when the Hypergeometric distribution can be used to model a scenario.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Identifying when the Hypergeometric distribution can be approximated by the Binomial distribution.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Using the Hypergeometric probability distribution function to compute probabilities.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Using the expected value and variance formulas for a Hypergeometric random variable.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Exercises Interactive Exercises

    1. Is this Hypergeometric?

    Which of the following random variables \(X\) can be modeled using a Hypergeometric distribution? Select all that apply.
    🔗
    • A shipping crate contains \(30\) ceramic mugs, of which \(5\) have hairline cracks. A quality inspector randomly selects a sample of \(6\) mugs from the crate. Let \(X\) be the number of cracked mugs in the sample.
    • Correct! We are drawing a fixed sample of size \(n = 6\) without replacement (implied) from a finite population of \(N = 30\) mugs containing \(A = 5\) cracked mugs (“successes”) and \(B=25\) ok mugs, and we are counting how many successes appear in the sample.
    • A university department has \(5\) statisticians, \(3\) data scientists, \(9\) mathematicians, and \(7\) computer scientists. A curriculum committee of \(4\) faculty members is formed by selecting names at random from a hat. Let \(X\) be the number of statisticians chosen for the committee.
    • Correct! The population consists of \(N = 24\) faculty members, which can be split into two groups (\(A = 5\) statisticians and \(B=19\) other faculty), and we draw a sample of \(n = 4\) individuals without replacement (implied) while counting the number from the first group in the sample.
    • A bag contains \(12\) red marbles and \(18\) blue marbles. A student draws a marble at random, records its color, and places it back into the bag before shaking and drawing again. This is repeated \(8\) times. Let \(X\) be the number of red marbles drawn.
    • Incorrect. Because each marble is returned to the bag before the next draw, the trials are independent with a constant success probability (\(p = 12/30 = 0.4\)). This describes a Binomial random variable, rather than a Hypergeometric one.
    • A standard \(52\)-card deck is thoroughly shuffled, and cards are dealt one at a time without replacement until the \(2\text{nd}\) Heart is revealed. Let \(X\) be the total number of cards dealt.
    • Incorrect. While this involves sampling without replacement from a finite deck, the sample size is random — we stop upon reaching \(2\) successes, rather than taking a fixed sample of size \(n\text{.}\)
    • At a large automated bottling plant, \(2\%\) of all bottles produced are underfilled, independently from bottle to bottle. An auditor pulls \(25\) bottles from the continuous production line to test them. Let \(X\) be the number of underfilled bottles in the sample.
    • Incorrect. The bottles are produced independently from a continuous process with a constant defect rate \(p = 0.02\text{,}\) rather than being drawn without replacement from a small, fixed finite batch. This follows a Binomial distribution.
    Hint.
    To determine if a variable fits the hypergeometric model, check for these three features:
    🔗
    • Binary - Can the population be divided into two mutually exclusive groups with \(A\) items classified as Type I (or "successes") and \(B\) items classified as Type II (or "failures"), where the total population size is \(N=A+B?\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Fixed sample size - Is a sample with a fixed size, \(n \le N\text{,}\) drawn from the population?
      🔗
      🔗
    • Sampling without replacement - When the sample is drawn, are items selected without replacement, so trials are not independent and the probability of success changes from draw to draw?
      🔗
      🔗

    Let the random variable \(T\) represent the total combined time (in minutes) required to complete the installation of all 5 selected tracking modules. Find the standard deviation for the expected installation time.
    🔗
    • \(5.364\) minutes
    • Correct! First, determine the variance of X:
      \begin{equation*} V(X) = n \cdot \left(\frac{A}{A+B}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{B}{A+B}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{A+B-n}{A+B-1}\right) = 5 \cdot \left(\frac{2}{20}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{18}{20}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{15}{19}\right)\approx 0.355263 \end{equation*}
      Next, define the linear time function, \(T\text{.}\) Each of the \(X\) defective units takes 10 minutes, while each of the remaining \((5 - X)\) working units takes 1 minute:
      \begin{equation*} T = 10X + 1(5 - X) = 9X + 5 \end{equation*}
      Then, by our variance properties, we have
      \begin{equation*} V(T) = V(9X + 5) = 9^2V(X) =81(0.355263) \approx 28.776\text{.} \end{equation*}
      Last, take the square root to get the standard deviation of 5.364 minutes.

    Exercises Written Exercises

    Recall that the binomial distribution is used when we want to calculate the likelihood that an event happens a certain number of times during \(n\) independent Bernoulli trials. The hypergeometric model handles dependent trials (e.g., sampling without replacement).
    🔗
    However, the binomial distribution can be used to approximate the hypergeometric distribution in certain circumstances. To better understand this approximation, answer the questions that follow.
    🔗

    1. Capture-Recapture Scenario.

    Suppose there are 1,000 fish in a lake. During the first week, 65 fish are captured, tagged, and released. Then, 30 fish are captured two weeks later. Let \(X\) be a hypergeometric random variable representing the number of tagged fish found in the sample of size 30.
    🔗

    (a)

    What is the probability that 3 of the 30 fish will be tagged? In other words, use the hypergeometric probability distribution to find \(P(X=3)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Consider the second sample of size 30. Compute each of the following probabilities. Write your answer as a decimal rounded to four decimal places.
    🔗
    Remember, the sample is taken without replacement.
    🔗
    (i)
    What is the probability that the first fish captured is tagged?
    🔗
    🔗
    (ii)
    Suppose that the first fish was tagged. What is the probability that the second fish captured is also tagged?
    🔗
    🔗
    (iii)
    Suppose that the first fish was tagged but the second fish was not. What is the probability that the third fish captured is tagged?
    🔗
    🔗
    (iv)
    Suppose 29 fish have been caught and that two of these fish were tagged. What is the probability that the last fish captured (number 30 in our sample) is tagged?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Referring to part (b), explain why these trials can be considered Bernoulli trials but not independent and identically distributed Bernoulli trials (which we need in order to use the Binomial distribution).

    (d)

    Because the sample size is small relative to the size of the population (30 vs 1,000), the probabilities you calculated in part (b) are quite similar!
    🔗
    Is it therefore reasonable to just fix the probability of success at \(p = \frac{65}{1000} = 0.065\) for all 30 trials (and pretend they are independent)? If so, this would allow us to use the binomial distribution!
    🔗
    Determine whether the binomial distribution can provide an accurate approximation for the probability in part (a). In other words, re-compute the probability that exactly 3 of the 30 fish will be tagged using the binomial distribution with \(p = \frac{65}{1000} = 0.065\text{.}\) Then, compare your answer to your initial answer using the hypergeometric distribution.
    🔗

    (e)

    In general, if the sample size is at most 5% of the population, then we can use the binomial distribution to approximate the hypergeometric.
