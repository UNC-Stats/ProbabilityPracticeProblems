    1. Continuous Distributions: Statistics Quiz Completion Times.

    The amount of time it takes a MATH 350 student to complete a probability exam can be modeled by a continuous uniform distribution between 25 and 55 minutes. If one students is selected completely at random from the class, compute the following probabilities for that student.
    🔗
    (a)
    Find the probability that the student requires more than 51 minutes to complete the exam (\(P(X \gt 51)\)).
    🔗
    • \(0.8667\)
    • Incorrect. This is the probability that the student takes less than 51 minutes (\(P(X \le 51)\)). Remember to find the area of the upper tail corresponding to "more than."
    • \(0.1333\)
    • Correct! For a uniform distribution on the interval \([a, b] = [25, 55]\text{,}\) the total width is \(b - a = 55 - 25 = 30\) minutes. The target width for taking more than 51 minutes spans from 51 to 55:
      \begin{equation*} P(X \gt 51) = \frac{55 - 51}{55 - 25} = \frac{4}{30} \approx 0.1333 \end{equation*}
    • \(0.0727\)
    • Incorrect. It looks like you divided the target width of 4 minutes by the upper boundary of 55 instead of the actual total width of the distribution support interval (\(30\) minutes).
    • \(0.1600\)
    • Incorrect. This distractor assumes a baseline width calculation slip where the interval was mistakenly calculated out of 25 instead of 30. Check your \(b - a\) subtraction.
    • \(0.0667\)
    • Incorrect. This matches only a 2-minute interval width over the 30-minute support space. Review the limits bounding the upper tail area.
    🔗
    (b)
    Find the probability that the student completes the exam in a time frame between 31 and 35 minutes (\(P(31 \le X \le 35)\)).
    🔗
    • \(0.1333\)
    • Correct! The length of the target sub-interval is \(d - c = 35 - 31 = 4\) minutes. Dividing this by the total distribution support width of 30 minutes gives:
      \begin{equation*} P(31 \le X \le 35) = \frac{35 - 31}{55 - 25} = \frac{4}{30} \approx 0.1333 \end{equation*}
      Notice that because the uniform distribution has a constant flat probability density, any sub-interval of width 4 within the main domain boundaries has an identical likelihood weight.
    • \(0.2000\)
    • Incorrect. This looks like a mistake where a width of 5 minutes was used in the numerator (\(35 - 30\)) instead of evaluating the true net difference between 35 and 31.
    • \(0.1143\)
    • Incorrect. It looks like you divided the sub-interval width of 4 by the upper bound of 35 instead of using the total uniform interval width parameter (\(30\)).
    • \(0.3333\)
    • Incorrect. This matches a full 10-minute interval block over the support range. Re-verify your boundary subtraction steps.
    • \(0.1000\)
    • Incorrect. This matches a 3-minute sub-interval step. The distance between 31 minutes and 35 minutes is exactly 4 minutes.
    🔗

    2. Continuous Distributions: Greeley Winter High Temperatures.

    Historical climate records indicate that the daily daytime high temperature during the month of December in Greeley, Colorado follows a continuous uniform distribution over the interval from 32 to 52 degrees Fahrenheit. A business traveler arrives in the city on a randomly selected day in December.
    🔗
    (a)
    What is the probability that the daytime high temperature will be above 43 degrees Fahrenheit (\(P(X \gt 43)\))?
    🔗
    • \(0.5500\)
    • Incorrect. This is the probability that the high temperature is below 43 degrees (\(P(X \le 43)\)). Remember to isolate the upper portion of the interval for "above."
    • \(0.4500\)
    • Correct! The total width of the uniform distribution support is \(b - a = 52 - 32 = 20\) degrees. The target interval width for high temperatures above 43 degrees spans from 43 to 52:
      \begin{equation*} P(X \gt 43) = \frac{52 - 43}{52 - 32} = \frac{9}{20} = 0.4500 \end{equation*}
    • \(0.1731\)
    • Incorrect. It looks like you divided the target width of 9 by the upper bound of 52 instead of using the total uniform interval width parameter (\(20\)).
    • \(0.4300\)
    • Incorrect. Do not confuse the boundary threshold value (43) with a probability ratio. Calculate the width of the target zone over the total zone width.
    • \(0.3500\)
    • Incorrect. Review your boundary subtraction steps. The distance between 43 and 52 is exactly 9 units.
    🔗
    (b)
    What is the probability that the daily high temperature will be between 34 and 46 degrees Fahrenheit (\(P(34 \le X \le 46)\))?
    🔗
    • \(0.4000\)
    • Incorrect. This matches a target width of 8 units instead of the true distance between 34 and 46.
    • \(0.7000\)
    • Incorrect. This is the cumulative probability area to the left of the upper boundary (\(P(X \le 46)\)). You still need to subtract the lower tail area under 34 degrees.
    • \(0.6000\)
    • Correct! The length of our target sub-interval is \(d - c = 46 - 34 = 12\) degrees. Dividing this by the total distribution support width of 20 degrees gives:
      \begin{equation*} P(34 \le X \le 46) = \frac{46 - 34}{52 - 32} = \frac{12}{20} = 0.6000 \end{equation*}
    • \(0.1000\)
    • Incorrect. This is the lower tail probability of experiencing a high temperature cooler than 34 degrees (\(P(X \lt 34)\)).
    • \(0.2609\)
    • Incorrect. Make sure to divide the sub-interval distance by the true width parameter (\(b - a = 20\)) rather than an upper boundary score.

    1. Analytical Derivations of Mean and Variance.

    Let \(X\) be a continuous uniform random variable defined over the real-numbered interval \([A, B]\text{,}\) where \(A \lt B\text{.}\) The constant probability density function (PDF) is given by:
    \begin{equation*} f_X(x) = \frac{1}{B - A}, \quad \text{for } A \le x \le B \end{equation*}
    and \(f_X(x) = 0\) elsewhere. Complete the analytical derivations below to verify its expected value and variance.
    🔗
    (a)
    Part A: Prove that the expected value (mean) for a continuous uniform random variable on the interval \([A, B]\) is:
    \begin{equation*} E(X) = \frac{A + B}{2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Part B: Show that the mathematical variance for a continuous uniform random variable on the interval \([A, B]\) is given by the formula:
    \begin{equation*} V(X) = \frac{(B - A)^2}{12} \end{equation*}
