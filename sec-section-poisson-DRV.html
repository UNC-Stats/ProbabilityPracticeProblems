    1. Emergency Room Patient Intake.

    During a busy weekend shift, trauma patients arrive at an urban emergency room triage desk according to a Poisson process with a mean rate of 80 patients per hour. If the triage nurse steps away to grab a clean stethoscope, leaving the desk unattended for exactly one minute, what is the probability that at least 1 patient arrives during that brief interval?
    🔗
    • \(0.2636\)
    • Incorrect. This is the probability that zero patients arrive during the one-minute window (\(P(Y=0)\)). Remember to use the complement rule to find the probability of \(\text{at least } 1\) arrival.
    • \(0.7364\)
    • Correct! First, convert the hourly arrival rate to a 1-minute time block: \(\lambda = \frac{80 \text{ patients}}{60 \text{ minutes}} = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.3333\) arrivals per minute.
      Then, apply the complement rule:
      \begin{equation*} P(Y \ge 1) = 1 - P(Y = 0) = 1 - \frac{(1.3333)^0 e^{-1.3333}}{0!} = 1 - e^{-1.3333} \approx 1 - 0.2636 = 0.7364 \end{equation*}
    • \(0.3515\)
    • Incorrect. This is the probability that exactly one patient arrives (\(P(Y=1)\)). The question requires the probability of \(\text{at least } 1\text{,}\) which accounts for multiple simultaneous patient walk-ins.
    • \(0.5488\)
    • Incorrect. It looks like you didn’t properly scale your time interval or miscalculated the exponent. Double-check your calculation for converting 80 patients per hour down to a single minute.
    🔗

    2. Campus Security Patrol Allocation.

    Suppose that a campus security department devises a randomized patrol pattern to ensure unpredictability. A security vehicle may visit a high-priority quad location \(Y = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\) times per one-hour night shift block. Over a long observation window, records show each location is visited an average of once per hour. Assume that \(Y\) follows a Poisson probability distribution.
    🔗
    (a)
    How could you identify that \(Y\) is a Poisson random variable if that information had not been explicitly provided?
    🔗
    • The variable counts random, independent events occurring over a fixed continuum of time, where the rate is proportional to the interval size.
    • Correct! The Poisson distribution models the counts of independent occurrences over a continuous interval (like an hour) given a known average rate (\(\lambda = 1\) visit per hour).
    • The variable counts independent trials that can only result in binary success or failure outcomes from a fixed set of trials.
    • Incorrect. That description fits a Binomial distribution. Poisson distributions do not have a fixed upper limit of discrete trials.
    • The variable tracks the total number of consecutive trials required until the very first success is observed.
    • Incorrect. That description defines a Geometric random variable. Here, we are counting occurrences within a standard time block.
    • The visits have a uniform chance of happening at any point, meaning the count is steady.
    • Incorrect. Uniform distributions track spatial or temporal position variations, not accumulated integer incidence tallies. Also, \(\lambda\) is the mean rate, which is 1.
    • It tracks changing success probabilities across consecutive non-replacement choices.
    • Incorrect. That description applies to a Hypergeometric distribution, not a time-series event sequence.

    1. Analyzing Cryptographic Hashing Probabilities.

    Bitcoin mining utilizes an algorithmic process called cryptographic hashing, which structurally behaves like a sequence of millions of independent Bernoulli trials. Each time a computing node performs a single hash, it has a tiny probability of \(p = 1.15 \times 10^{-23}\) of successfully solving the current network difficulty puzzle. Solving a block rewards the miner with a subsidy of \(3.125\) Bitcoins.
    🔗
    These operations are executed by specialized Application-Specific Integrated Circuit (ASIC) computers called miners. A typical enterprise miner performs approximately \(9.3 \times 10^{13}\) hashes per second. Suppose a commercial operation deploys a farm array of \(10\) identical miners and runs them continuously for exactly one year.
    🔗
    (a)
    Determine the total cumulative number of hashes performed by the 10-miner farm array over the course of a full 365-day year.

    🔗
