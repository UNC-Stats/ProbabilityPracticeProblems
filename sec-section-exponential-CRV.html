    3. Proof Derivation: Expected Value of an Exponential Distribution.

    Let \(X\) be a continuous random variable following an exponential distribution with rate parameter \(\lambda\text{,}\) where its probability density function (PDF) is defined as \(f_X(x) = \lambda e^{-\lambda x}\) for \(x \ge 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Arrange the blocks below in the correct logical sequence to derive the expected value. Recall: \(E(X) = \frac{1}{\lambda}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Set up the integral definition for the expected value:
    
\begin{equation*}
E(X) = \int_{0}^{\infty} x \cdot \left(\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\right) \, dx
\end{equation*}

    
---


    Get ready to apply integration by parts! Assign your terms:
    
\begin{align*}
u = x \amp \implies du = dx\\
dv = \lambda e^{-\lambda x} \, dx \amp \implies v = -e^{-\lambda x}
\end{align*}

    
---


    Substitute these terms into the integration by parts formula \(\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du\text{:}\)

    
\begin{equation*}
E(X) = \left[ -x e^{-\lambda x} \right]_{0}^{\infty} - \int_{0}^{\infty} \left(-e^{-\lambda x}\right) \, dx
\end{equation*}

    
---


    Evaluate the limits for the first term \(uv\text{,}\) noticing that the upper limit goes to 0 by L’Hôpital’s Rule:
    
\begin{equation*}
E(X) = 0 + \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-\lambda x} \, dx
\end{equation*}

    
---


    Compute the remaining integral. First find the anti-derivative:
    
\begin{equation*}
E(X) = \left[ -\frac{1}{\lambda} e^{-\lambda x} \right]_{0}^{\infty} 
\end{equation*}

    
---


    Evaluate the anti-derivative from 0 to \(\infty\text{:}\)

    
\begin{equation*}
E(X) =  0 - \left( -\frac{1}{\lambda} \cdot 1 \right) = \frac{1}{\lambda}
\end{equation*}

    1. Auditing the Apex Bank Billboard Campaign.

    A regional mega-bank, desperate to steal customers from its competitors, launches a flashy billboard campaign asserting they have the absolute fastest tellers in the tri-state area. Their main marketing slogan claims that "virtually all of our customers are served in less than 10 minutes!"
    🔗
    An amused data scientist opens an account, sits in the lobby with a hidden stopwatch, and collects a large sample of service times. They conclude that the customer service times are actually exponentially distributed with a long-run mean of \(\mu = 5.5\) minutes. Let us investigate what the bank’s marketing department mathematically means by "virtually all."
    🔗
    (a)
    State the probability density function (PDF), \(f_X(x)\text{,}\) for this service model. Then use this PDF to compute the proportion of customers who are actually served in under 10 minutes.
    🔗
    Hint.
    This is the same as finding the probability that customers are served in under ten minutes!
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    State the cumulative distribution function (CDF), \(F_X(x)\text{,}\) for this service model. Use it to recompute the proportion of customers served in under 10 minutes.
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    While the bank’s average (mean) service time is 5.5 minutes, the exponential distribution is heavily skewed toward longer wait times. Find the median service time to determine how fast the middle customer (the 50th percentile) actually gets served.
