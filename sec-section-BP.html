    1. Three Children.

    Suppose that a couple has three children. We are concerned with the sex of the children at birth.
    🔗
    (a)
    Which option best describes the sample space here?
    🔗
    • \(\{BBB, GBB, BGB, BBG, GGB, GBG, BGG, GGG\} \)
    • Correct! This set provides a list of all possible outcomes.
    • \(\{BBB, GGG, BBG, GGB\} \)
    • This option does not take the order of the births into account.
    • \(\{B, G\} \)
    • This would be the sample space for the birth of a SINGLE child, not three children.

    • This would be the sample space if the couple were to have up to three children, not exactly three children.

    3. Streaming Movies and TV.

    Your professor wants to watch something but cannot decide on what. So, she first tosses a fair coin to choose a streaming platform. If the coin lands heads, she will log into Netflix (\(N\)); if it lands tails, she will log into Hulu (\(H\)).
    🔗
    Once logged in, she randomly selects content (e.g., tv show, movie, etc):
    • If she is on Netflix, she will choose randomly between a movie (\(M\)) or a documentary (\(D\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • If she is on Hulu, she will choose randomly between a comedy show (\(C\)), an anime series (\(A\)), or a reality TV episode (\(R\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    The "experiment" is to observe the combination of the streaming platform and specific content type chosen.
    🔗
    (a)
    Which of the following sets correctly describes the full sample space \(S\) for this experiment?
    🔗
    • \(\{(N, M), (N, D), (H, C), (H, A)\}\)
    • Incorrect. This subset leaves out the outcome \((H, R)\) for watching a reality TV episode on Hulu. A complete sample space must list every possible outcome.
    • \(\{(N, M), (N, D), (N, C), (N, A), (N, R), (H, M), (H, D), (H, C), (H, A), (H, R)\}\)
    • Incorrect. This list includes outcomes that are not possible! For example, she will not select a comedy show on Netflix.
    • \(\{(N, M), (N, D), (H, C), (H, A), (H, R)\}\)
    • Correct! This set contains all 5 possible outcomes.
    • \(\{N, H, M, D, C, A, R\}\)
    • Incorrect. This is a list of individual choices rather than combinations. The "experiment" is to observe the combination of the streaming platform AND specific content type chosen.
    • \(\{(N, H), (M, D), (C, A, R)\}\)
    • Incorrect. This lists groupings of the possible individual choices, rather than the possible combinations of streaming platform AND content.
    🔗
    (b)
    Let \(E\) be the event that the professor chooses to watch a fictional scripted story (defined here as a movie, a comedy show, or an anime series). Which subset correctly defines the event \(E\text{?}\)
    🔗
    • \(\{M, C, A\}\)
    • Incorrect. This list contains only the individual content options. \(E\) should be a subset of outcomes from the sample space.
    • \(\{(N, M), (H, C), (H, A)\}\)
    • Correct! The subset \(E\) filters the total sample space \(S\) to capture only the outcomes matching our event definition.
    • \(\{(N, M), (N, D), (H, C), (H, A), (H, R)\}\)
    • Incorrect. This is the entire sample space \(S\text{,}\) but not all outcomes in the sample space belong to event \(E\text{.}\)
    • \(\{(H, C), (H, A)\}\)
    • Incorrect. This subset is missing an outcome: the movie on Netflix.
    • \(\{N, D), (H, R)\}\)
    • Incorrect. This subset captures the exact opposite of event \(E\text{!}\)
    🔗
    (c)
      All outcomes in the sample space are equally likely.
      🔗

    • True.

    • The multi-stage structure of the experiment affects the probabilities of the sample space outcomes. After the initial coin flip, there are different numbers of subsequent choices.

    • False.

    • The multi-stage structure of the experiment affects the probabilities of the sample space outcomes. After the initial coin flip, there are different numbers of subsequent choices.
